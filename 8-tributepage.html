Tribute to Squadron Leader Ajay Ahuja

Squadron Leader Ajay Ahuja (1963–1999) was an Indian Air Force fighter pilot and Kargil War hero who was posthumously awarded the Vir Chakra for his bravery. ⁠ Born in Kota, Rajasthan, he was commissioned in 1985 and served as the Flight Commander of No. 17 Squadron ("Golden Arrows"). ⁠ On May 27, 1999, during Operation Safed Sagar, his MiG-21 was hit by a Pakistani Stinger missile while he was conducting a reconnaissance mission. ⁠ He ejected and landed in Pakistani-occupied Kashmir, where he was captured, tortured, and killed. India accused Pakistani forces of violating the Geneva Conventions by executing him after his capture, a claim Pakistan denied.

Military: No. 17 Squadron IAF — The Golden Arrows is the nickname for No. 17 Squadron of the Indian Air Force (IAF), formed on October 1, 1951, at Ambala Air Force Station. ⁠ Historically known for its role in the Kargil War (1999) flying MiG-21s for reconnaissance, the squadron was revived in 2019 as the IAF’s first Rafale multirole fighter squadron. ⁠ Its motto is Udayam Ajasram ("Ever Rising Skywards").
